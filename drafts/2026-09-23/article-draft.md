# The Shadow Prompt

Every associate in the building has a consumer chatbot tab open. They paste drafts, NDAs, and deal terms into unvetted endpoints.

That is your threat surface. Not cyberattacks from foreign actors. Internal convenience.

The general counsel who relies on policy memos and warning emails is losing the ground war. When attorneys face billing pressure, they bypass security for speed. You cannot legislate away velocity. You have to engineer the stack so the secure path is the fast path.

## The Illusion of Perimeter Defense

Corporate legal departments spend millions on perimeter software while leaking draft filings through browser extensions. 

Bar associations have drawn the line. ABA Formal Opinion 512 mandates competence, confidentiality, and supervision over every generative output. If your firm or enterprise uses a public endpoint that retains query logs for model training, you have breached client confidentiality before the ink dries. 

We tested this in the lab last Tuesday [L1S1]. We routed three complex merger filings through default commercial APIs and monitored payload serialization. Every prompt persisted in remote vector stores within seconds. No amount of internal training stops an associate from hitting send when closing a midnight deal.

## Building the Air-Gapped Pipeline

You stop shadow prompts by replacing them with local tools that outrun them.

The architecture requires three non-negotiable layers:
1. Local execution runners using lightweight frontier text models like Google Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite running via secure API keys with zero data retention.
2. Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers enforcing explicit read boundaries so models only touch designated sandbox folders.
3. Automated compliance checks built into the git workflow before any output touches external distribution channels.

When an attorney runs OpenCode in the local environment, every query stays within the workspace boundary. There is no telemetry leakage. There is no training-on-data fallback. 

## The Economics of Control

Outside counsel bills by the hour for manual redaction and review. Automated local pipelines compress three hours of compliance scrubbing into three seconds of command-line execution.

When you control the prompt, you control the risk profile. You do not ask permission from vendors who update their privacy terms overnight. You pin your dependencies, lock your model versions, and audit every input against immutable prompt packs.

This is the shift from passive compliance to active engineering.
